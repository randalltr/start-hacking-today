# Introduction

I was once where you are now. 

I wanted to hack but I did not know how. Ever since watching the movie *Hackers* at 12-years-old I knew this was my calling, but I believed hacking required something I did not possess. I thought real hackers were different from me. I assumed they were naturally gifted, technical from birth, or somehow wired differently.

Decades passed and that belief stayed with me.

Then in my mid 30s I realized something uncomfortable. I would regret dying and never at least failing at the thing I felt I was born to do. That realization mattered more than fear or doubt. I decided I had to try, even if I failed publicly, even if I struggled, and even if it took years.

So I installed Kali Linux and stopped.

Then I installed it again and stopped once again.

And again.

Each time I opened it, I felt overwhelmed. I remember sitting there staring at hundreds of tools and having no idea what any of them actually did. I remember conversations with friends where we would say, “Look at all the tools in Kali. How do we even get started?”

This book answers that question. This book will make you a hacker as simply and efficiently as possible.

This book is not here to teach you everything about hacking. That is impossible. The internet already contains more information than anyone could learn in a lifetime. The real problem is not lack of information. The problem is knowing where to begin without drowning.

By the time you finish this book, you will know what you are doing. More importantly, you will understand why you are doing it. You will stop randomly clicking tools and start following a process. And yes, you will be dangerous. Dangerous not because you know everything, but because you finally know where to start and how to move forward.

I am not an expert. I am currently working toward earning the OSCP certification, a certification that separates the hobbyist from the professional hacker. I have also earned the AIRTP+, the highest AI hacking certification. I also completed a Masters in Cybersecurity. But none of this came easily:

- I failed the Pentest+ the first time I took it.
- I was kicked out of the AIRTP+ class the first time I attended.
- I failed many classes in high school and university but somehow graduated.
- I failed the OSCP the first time I attempted it, and I am coming back for more!

Failure is part of this path. Hacking is not a straight line. Progress does not feel smooth or predictable. Some days you feel unstoppable. Other days you feel completely lost again. The difference between people who eventually succeed and people who quit is not intelligence. It is resolve. I kept going. I continue to keep going and so should you.

Resolve will take you further than talent, background, or natural ability. If you stay consistent long enough, confusion slowly turns into familiarity. Commands begin to make sense. Outputs stop looking like noise. Patterns appear. You start recognizing what matters. At some point, hacking stops feeling like magic and starts feeling like investigation. This book is designed to accelerate that moment for you.

We are going to ignore most tools. We are going to ignore complicated theory. We are going to focus on a small set of skills that produce real results. You will learn three tools that form the foundation of practical hacking:

- **Nmap** - to see the target's attack surface
- **ExploitDB** - to gain access or escalate privileges
- **Burp Suite** - to become the man-in-the-middle of web communications

That is enough to begin.

You do not need to master Linux first. You do not need to memorize networking textbooks. You do not need to understand everything before starting. You only need curiosity and persistence.

You will get stuck. Everyone does. When that happens, use AI. Use YouTube. Search constantly. Real hackers look things up every day. There is no prize for memorizing commands. The skill is knowing how to learn and how to keep moving.

This book is an invitation to start today. You are ready. Join me.
